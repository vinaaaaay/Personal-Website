my patron saint



Diogenes of Sinope, roughly 404–323 BC. He lived in a big clay jar in
the Athens marketplace, owned almost nothing, and treated every custom
as a question nobody had bothered to ask.



    
  
  • Plato defined a human as “a featherless biped.” Diogenes plucked a chicken,
      brought it to Plato's school and said: “Here is Plato's human.”
      Plato quietly added “with broad flat nails” to the definition.
    • 
  
  • He walked around in broad daylight carrying a lit lamp. Asked why,
      he said he was looking for a human being. (Later retellings made it
      “an honest man.”)
    • 
  
  • Alexander the Great, the most powerful man alive, came to see him and
      offered him anything he wanted. Diogenes asked him to stand a little
      out of his sunlight.
    • 
  
  • Asked where he was from, he said: “I am a citizen of the world.”
    • 
  
  • His motto came from an oracle: deface the currency. Take the stamp
      society puts on things, and check whether the thing underneath is real.
    • 




All of this is from Diogenes Laërtius, Lives of Eminent Philosophers, Book VI.
Some of it is probably exaggerated. I like it more for that.
